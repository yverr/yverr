A Reader's Perspective on What Makes a Novel Successful and What Sustains Its Fandom

As someone who has entered thousands of worlds inside novels and has encountered many different types of stories. I'd like to leave behind a few thoughts for authors from the perspective of a reader who wants to discover more great novels, see more fan creations, and watch more thriving literary communities grow!

1. The quality of your writing matters, but initial impression is also important for a successful novel:

A novel's writing may be what keeps readers invested, but the novel's cover might be the reason why people will pick it up to read in the first place.

Research on book covers and reader perceptions suggests that visual presentation and perceived authenticity can influence how readers evaluate a book. Reader attitudes toward AI-generated covers also appear to vary, with some expressing strong reservations about them. However, the extent to which AI-generated covers affect actual readership and sales remains an empirical question; survey responses and stated preferences do not necessarily translate into reading behavior.

My concern is that an author may unintentionally discourage potential readers before they even encounter the story itself. That's why I believe that we should also consider the important of a novel's cover.

2. A thriving fandom can become a source of continued discovery.

After a reader has finished reading a novel, that hardly means that he or she has departed the world of the characters. There are cases when readers are so carried away by the book, its characters, and the story that they start creating things themselves.

Possible example of the cycle:

Interesting characters and plot → reader involvement → creating fan art and other things → increasing popularity → new readers.

This is a hypothesis of how fan creations in literature can continue the process of discovering the book for its audience but not a rule. Popularity can boost fans’ activities as well, which means that the connection between the two may occur in both directions.

However, the main value of fan-created work is that it makes it possible for the book to be read by a larger number of people than it would have otherwise been. Illustrations, character transformations, memes, videos, and discussions help announce the book to those who have never seen its cover or read its summary.

Official artwork may contribute to defining the book’s visual style and inspiring artists in their turn; fan art may expand the visual identity of the novel beyond the limits of the author.

3. Strong characters give readers something to become invested in.

Visual appeal alone cannot sustain a meaningful literary community indefinitely. There needs to be something further to invest in.

Is there sufficient depth in the characters to create multiple interpretations, emotional reactions, discussion, and creative output-to motivate the reader to linger long after he has finished the last chapter?

A compelling character can become the subject of illustrations, edits, theories, discussions, and countless interpretations. In that sense, character writing can contribute to the reading experience as well as the creative breath to the fandom life surrounding any novel.

This is not to say that every successful novel needs a large fan-art community. Rather, strong characterization creates ways for readers to connect with the story above and beyond just reading it.

4. Consider Lord of Mysteries and Omniscient Reader's Viewpoint.

These novels illustrate the kind of literary communities I am talking about: stories with recognizable characters, substantial readerships, and communities in which fan-created content can become part of the broader experience of following the series.

They are useful examples to investigate when considering the relationship between storytelling, visual presentation, and fandom. However, their success alone cannot establish that official artwork or fan art caused their popularity. Their writing, publication history, distribution, marketing, and existing readerships may also contribute.

5. What about Shadow Slave?

Shadow Slave offers a compelling case that challenges our notion the novel must have an official cover created by a human in order to become popular.

One possible explanation is that many readers discover the story without recognizing that its cover is AI-generated. As they continue reading, they may feel the involvement of the story and the characters, which may consequently lead to the rise or contribution to the fandom. Fan-created illustrations and other content may then help the novel reach additional readers.

This proposed sequence is plausible, but it has not been established as the actual explanation for Shadow Slave's success. Its popularity could reflect multiple factors, and determining the role of its cover would require evidence about reader awareness, initial discovery, and subsequent engagement.

Nevertheless, the example raises an interesting possibility: a novel's official artwork may influence its initial reception without determining the ultimate size or longevity of its fandom.

A strong story and an invested community may create opportunities for a novel to grow beyond its initial presentation.

6. My conclusion

I believe three elements deserve particular attention when authors think about the success of their novels:

Writing quality: The storytelling, worldbuilding, and character development that give readers reasons to stay.

Visual presentation: The cover and artwork that shape a novel's first impression and help establish its identity.

Community engagement: The opportunities for readers to express their investment through discussion, fan art, edits, videos, and other creations.

I would not claim that all three are necessary for every successful novel, nor that any one of them guarantees success. Different stories and audiences have different needs.

However, I believe authors should consider the relationship between these elements rather than treating writing and visual presentation as entirely separate concerns.

A novel can attract attention through its cover, retain readers through its storytelling, and gain further visibility through the community that forms around it. These processes can reinforce one another, although their relative importance will vary from one novel to another.

As a reader, I want to discover more stories worth loving, characters worth remembering, and communities in which that love can be expressed creatively.

That is ultimately why I believe visual presentation and fandom deserve more consideration in discussions about what makes a novel successful.

Thank you for reading!
